How this was measured

Commercial revenue counted only where the first touch appears in our outbound records, and recognised on progress billing rather than on contract value at signing. Residential is excluded from the share in full.

Situation

NSP ran on residential callouts and small remodels — high volume, thin margin, and entirely dependent on whoever answered the phone that morning. They wanted commercial and light industrial work, which does not arrive through the channels that bring in a tripped breaker at nine at night, and had no relationships with the general contractors who award it.

Strategy

Our fee came out of the new commercial revenue, which mattered more here than anywhere else we work. Commercial is slow. A retainer agency would have been paid handsomely through the eight months before any of it landed. We were not.

Residential was left alone; it paid the bills while the rest was built. Commercial ran as a named-account outbound motion against general contractors and facilities managers inside a sixty-mile radius, measured in bids invited rather than leads generated, because an electrical subcontract is won off a bid list you have to get onto first.

Results

Revenue growth of +50% across 11 months, on a business doing high seven figures. Our fee was A single-digit share of tracked new revenue and nothing else — no retainer, no monthly minimum, and nothing at all for the months before the number moved.

“Eight months in I had paid them almost nothing and I was starting to feel bad about it. Then the first two bids landed and I understood the arrangement.”

Curtis Hallberg, owner, NSP
