How this was measured

Profit share on net membership revenue against a trailing twelve-month baseline, so the seasonal comparison holds. Personal training revenue is excluded.

Situation

Westbrook was adding around ninety members a month and losing nearly as many. Every acquisition dollar was going into a bucket with a hole in it, and the owner was convinced the answer was a bigger acquisition budget.

Strategy

We refused to run acquisition for the first sixty days. On profit share that refusal cost us as much as it cost them, which is the only reason it was a credible thing to say out loud.

Onboarding was rebuilt around the first six weeks, where almost all of the churn sat, and we did not touch paid media until the retention curve moved. Then we scaled acquisition into a business that could hold what it caught.

Results

Gross profit growth of +60% across 8 months, on a business doing low seven figures. Our fee was A mid-teens share of incremental gross profit and nothing else — no retainer, no monthly minimum, and nothing at all for the months before the number moved.

“They spent two months telling me not to give them money. Then the number moved.”

Priya Raghunathan, founder, Westbrook Wellness
