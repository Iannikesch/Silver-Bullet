How this was measured

A quarter is the round number; the monthly reconciliation is not. Growth is counted on invoiced work rather than booked work, sourced to a tracked number or a channel-tagged form, and reconciled against their service management system. Maintenance agreements already in place at signing were carved out of the baseline, so renewals cannot flatter it.

Situation

At around $50M a year, Vitality is the largest firm we work with and the one with the least to prove. Licences, trucks, a twenty-year name in commercial mechanical across metro Detroit. What they did not have was a way to fill next month. Work arrived when something broke, so a strong quarter was followed by three weeks of technicians washing vans. Two previous agencies had been paid a retainer to fix it. Neither took a position on whether it worked.

Strategy

We would not take a retainer, and we said so on the first call. Our fee came out of tracked new revenue, which meant an empty calendar cost us what it cost them.

The work split in two. Emergency demand already existed, so we took it: paid search against failure intent, a tracked line per channel, and a dispatcher script that stopped qualified calls dying on hold. The harder half was planned maintenance, which is where a mechanical contractor's margin actually lives. We built a quoting path for annual agreements and gave the field techs a reason to raise it at the end of a call.

Results

Revenue growth of +25% across 9 months, on a business doing around $50M a year. Our fee was A single-digit share of tracked new revenue and nothing else — no retainer, no monthly minimum, and nothing at all for the months before the number moved.

“Two agencies before this one sent me a report every month. These guys sent me an invoice that only went up when my revenue did. That is a completely different conversation.”

Robert Ainsley, president, Vitality Mechanical
